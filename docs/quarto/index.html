Impact of Insurance on Utilization and Outcomes of Leadless Pacemakers: A Nationwide Analysis From 2016 to 2020

Analysis for Broad Peak

Author
Affiliation

Mariam Shahabi

Dow University of Health Sciences

Preamble:

  • Reference Papers:

    • Alhuarrat et al. 2023
    • Agarwal et al. 2025
    • Nguyen et al. 2025

  • Study Objective: To analyze the impact of insurance on in-hospital mortality, complications, and resource utilization in individuals undergoing pacemaker implantation in U.S. hospitals from 2016 to 2020, adjusting for demographic, socioeconomic, clinical, and hospital-related factors.

  • Data Source: Cross-sectional analysis using the National Inpatient Sample (NIS) database from 2016 to 2020, a representative sample of all-payer inpatient hospitalizations in the U.S.

  • Patient Selection: Included all inpatient admissions for patients 18 years or older with a primary or secondary procedure code of leadless pacemaker implantation, identified using the ICD-10 diagnosis code 02HK3NZ. Patients were then stratified by insurance status as Private, Medicare, or Medicaid.

  • Outcomes of Interest:

    • In-hospital Mortality

    • Length of Stay (days)

    • Inflation-adjusted Total Charge ($) [adjusted to 2020 dollars, using CPI data by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics]

    • Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

    • Any Complication (including vascular, pericardial, pneumothorax, infectious, or device-related complications)

  • Statistical Analysis: Univariable and multivariable analyses were conducted to evaluate the association between insurance and inpatient outcomes within the leadless pacemaker cohort:

    • Univariable Analysis:

      • Continuous variables: Design-based Kruskal-Wallis test.

      • Categorical variables: Pearson’s X² test with Rao & Scott adjustment to account for survey design.

    • Multivariable Analysis:

      • Logistic regression for binary outcomes (e.g., in-hospital mortality, AKI).

      • Linear regression for continuous outcomes (e.g., length of stay, total charges).

    • Adjustments: Models controlled for the following covariates:

      • Demographics and Socioeconomic Factors: Age, Sex, Race, Residential income, Expected primary payer.

      • Clinical Factors: Charlson comorbidity index, Admission Type, Chronic Kidney Disease, Hypertension, Diabetes Mellitus, Obesity, Chronic Pulmonary Disease, Liver Disease, Peripheral Vascular Disease, Congestive Heart Failure, Myocardial Infarction.

      • Hospital-Level Factors: Hospital region, Hospital bedsize, Hospital location and teaching status.

  • Software: All statistical analyses were performed using R Statistical Language (Version 4.5.0; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), incorporating survey-weighted procedures via the survey package to account for the complex sampling design of NIS.
